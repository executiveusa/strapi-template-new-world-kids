# Paid work for 16 to 19 year olds: compliance checklist (draft for counsel)

Source: Washington Department of Labor and Industries (lni.wa.gov), pages read 2026-10-09.
**Non-agricultural child labor rules (chapter 296-125 WAC) changed on July 1, 2026.** Re-check before launch.
Federal rules also apply. Where state and federal differ, follow the stricter one.

## Who is covered

- 16 and 17: minors. All rules below apply.
- 18 and 19: adults for work-hour rules. Keep them on the same project safety standard.
- Volunteers are outside the child labor rules, but participants here are paid, so they are employees.

## Before any 16 or 17 year old starts (L&I "How to hire minors")

1. **Minor work permit endorsement** on the employer's business license through the Department of Revenue,
   for every location where minors work. (Which entity is the employer: New World Kids, HSI, or the host?)
2. **Parent/School Authorization form** (F700-002-000) per minor, signed by employer, minor, school official,
   and parent or guardian. Expires every September 30. Summer-only: F700-168-000.
3. **Proof of age**: birth certificate, driver's license, passport, or notarized parent statement.
4. Emancipated minors do not need the form but still need the endorsement.

## Hours (non-agricultural, 16 to 17)

| Week                              | Per day | Per week | Days | Window             |
| --------------------------------- | ------- | -------- | ---- | ------------------ |
| School week                       | 4 hours | 20 hours | 6    | 7 a.m. to 10 p.m.  |
| Non-school week                   | 8 hours | 48 hours | 6    | 5 a.m. to midnight |
| School week with special variance | 6 hours | 28 hours | 6    | 7 a.m. to 10 p.m.  |

- Minors cannot work during their neighborhood public school's hours unless an exception applies
  (alternative, private, online or home school, a CTE worksite learning program, or superintendent excuse).
  Weeks with any school time are school weeks.
- Exemptions to non-school-week hours include a diploma or GED, college enrollment (Running Start),
  being a parent, or CTE/Core Plus enrollment. Keep proof.
- **Youth who are not enrolled in school still follow the hours limits.** A Minor Work Variance from L&I
  exists for 16 and 17 year olds who are not enrolled or need more hours.
- Meal break: no more than 5 consecutive hours without one (WAC 296-125-121). Confirm rest-break rules.
- Service work after 8 p.m. needs a responsible adult on the premises.
- Overtime applies over 40 hours a week.

## Prohibited duties (anyone under 18), including

Wrecking, demolition, mechanical excavation; roofing or work on or around a roof; operating earth-moving
equipment; power-driven woodworking machines, circular and band saws, chainsaws; power-driven metal forming
or shearing. Full list: lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/youth-employment/prohibited-duties. Screen **every
project** against it, and against the federal Hazardous Occupations Orders (dol.gov/youthrules).
This matters most for the Ground Up (construction, site improvement) and Make Your Mark (wall preparation) pathways.

## Employer duties to confirm with counsel

- Wage: the higher of state and local minimum wage for the worksite.
- Workers' compensation (industrial insurance) coverage and general liability insurance.
- Payroll: who runs it, and can HSI contracted services act as payroll or employer of record?
- Record retention and postings.
- Complaint path: anyone can file a child labor complaint with L&I.
