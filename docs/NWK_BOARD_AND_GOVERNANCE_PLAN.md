# Board and governance plan (draft for the founder, counsel, and HSI)

Checked against public sources on 2026-10-09. Not legal advice.

## What the law says (verified)

- RCW 24.03A.505: a Washington nonprofit board has **one or more** directors, but must have **three or more**
  if the IRS has determined the corporation is a public charity (section 509(a)), or it has applied for that status.
  New World Kids has no IRS determination of its own today (HSI holds the 501(c)(3)). So one governor is legal now.
  Directors under 18 may not exceed three or one third of the board.
- The Washington record (checked 2026-10-09) lists one governor: Jeremy Bowers. He is also the registered agent.
- HSI agreement section 9: tell HSI immediately of any change in executive or key staff.

## Why add people anyway

One person is governor, registered agent, program manager and signatory. Funders, HSI, and parents read
that as a single point of failure and a conflict of interest. Three unrelated people fixes most of it.

## Minimum board: 3 seats (target 5)

| Seat                                  | Who to look for                                                                                                                                              | Why                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Chair                              | Jeremy Bowers (founder)                                                                                                                                      | Holds the mission                                                         |
| 2. Treasurer                          | An independent person who has kept books or run a nonprofit budget (bookkeeper, CPA, nonprofit finance staff). Not a relative, not paid by the organization. | Signs off budgets and reports to HSI; separates money from the founder    |
| 3. Youth safety and workforce         | Someone from youth services: school counselor, youth worker, juvenile rehabilitation or DCYF alumnus, workforce development council staff                    | Chairs safeguarding; credible with referral partners in all four counties |
| 4. Labor and compliance (recommended) | HR or employment lawyer, or a pro bono adviser                                                                                                               | Owns the youth employment checklist                                       |
| 5. Lived experience (recommended)     | A young adult (18+) with foster care, justice, or housing experience                                                                                         | Keeps the program honest; stay clear of the under-18 director limit       |

The sports mentor lead is best as **program lead or adviser**. If they are paid, or paid in future, they
are not an independent director. Keep at least two directors who are unrelated and unpaid.

## Advisory circle (non-voting)

One mentor lead per pathway: technology, sports, food systems, art.

## Where to look

Washington Nonprofits (washingtonnonprofits.org) board resources; the workforce development council in each of
King, Snohomish, Pierce, and Skagit; youth-serving organizations you already work with. Names are yours to choose.

## To do once you have three people

1. Amend the bylaws: board size, terms, officers, quorum, conflict of interest. Have counsel review.
2. Adopt a conflict-of-interest policy and a safeguarding policy (draft in `docs/drafts/`).
3. File an updated Washington annual report with the new governors; tell HSI in writing (agreement section 9).
4. Add each person to `apps/ui/src/content/facts.ts` with their written permission.
